# Blast Agents clears costs, but users carry the risk

Blast Agents is profitable per vault at $0.99/day, but the SPEC 1b tables flatter it and the real constraint is the customer, not the cost line. The AI price inputs are correct: both `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` and `openai/gpt-6.1-sol` list at **$2 / $10 per 1M tokens** on OpenRouter, and OpenRouter charges no inference markup, only a **5% fee on crypto credit purchases**, so the plan's ~$0.018 per uncached decision checks out ($0.01785). Sui gas is cheap enough to ignore at the margin. Three things break the plan as written. First, triggers fire per launch per vault, so once Blast sees more than ~30 launches a day every vault sits at the 30-decision cap, which makes the "busy" day the _typical_ day. Second, the SPEC's own $0.022 cost alarm is set above the level at which its 30%-margin rule fails (about $0.0204), and the current trader code allows 2,000 output tokens per call, enough to make a capped day lose money. Third, a flat $0.99/day on the $250 minimum is a **144.5%/yr fee drag** in a market where half or more of memecoin wallets lose money in a typical month. That points to heavy churn, an LTV of roughly $45–$120 against a funded-user CAC of $100–$300, and break-even nearer 250–400 paid vaults than the SPEC's 185. The fixes are mostly engineering: score each launch once globally, enforce caching with a hit-rate alarm, cap output tokens, correct the alarm thresholds, and change pricing so small vaults get a cheaper model tier instead of a bigger fee drag. Regulation is the one cost that could swamp everything: if counsel classes the agent as MiCA portfolio management, Portuguese CASP authorisation costs roughly €300k–€700k in year one.

## Per-decision AI cost checks out at $0.018, with caching worth up to 50% more margin

The list prices in SPEC 1b and 6.1 are right as of November 2026. OpenRouter shows Sonnet 5.5 at **$2/M input and $10/M output** ([OpenRouter compare](https://openrouter.ai/compare/anthropic/claude-sonnet-5/anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5)), and GPT-6.1 Sol at the same rates, served by OpenAI and Azure, with a dated slug `openai/gpt-6.1-sol-20260929` ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-6.1-sol-20260929); [LLM Reference](https://www.llmreference.com/model/gpt-6.1-sol/openrouter)). OpenRouter "never marks up provider pricing". Its fee comes on credit purchases: **5.0% flat for crypto, 5.5% with a $0.80 minimum for card** ([OpenRouter blog](https://openrouter.ai/blog/simplifying-our-platform-fee)). At 6,000 input and 500 output tokens, one decision costs $0.0170 raw and **$0.01785 with the USDC fee**, so the SPEC's "about 6%" is a slightly conservative rounding. Two operating details matter. Crypto top-ups are non-refundable and credits can expire after 365 days ([OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)), so buy one to three months of runway at a time. And pin the dated GPT slug so that price and behaviour cannot drift silently.

Caching is where the margin is, but it is not free and not automatic. Sonnet 5.5's cache read was cut to **$0.10/M in October 2026** ([SmartScope](https://smartscope.blog/en/blog/claude-sonnet-5-5-cache-read-price-cut-2026/)), and a 5-minute cache write costs **$2.50/M, 1.25x input** ([Apidog](https://apidog.com/blog/claude-sonnet-5-5-pricing/)). GPT-6.1 Sol cached input is also $0.10/M, and third-party tables list a $2.50 write that OpenAI's own pages have not confirmed ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gpt-6-1-sol-pricing)). Treat the 5,000-token shared prefix as cacheable. A decision then costs:

| Prefix cache hit rate  | Cost per decision (incl. 5% fee) | vs. no caching |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Caching off            | $0.01785                         | baseline       |
| 0% (every call writes) | $0.02048                         | +15%           |
| 50%                    | $0.01418                         | −21%           |
| 80%                    | $0.01039                         | −42%           |
| 95%                    | $0.00851                         | −52%           |

Caching beats no caching only above a **~21% hit rate**, and the hit rate is set by how many decisions share one tick's snapshot. With k decisions per tick per model the ceiling is (k−1)/k. Spread evenly, 30 decisions/vault/day over 1,440 ticks gives k ≈ 2 at 100 vaults, which is roughly a 50% hit rate. Two models halve k again, because each provider keeps its own cache. Event triggers help here: a "new launch" trigger fires for every vault on the same tick, so k approaches the number of vaults on that model.

OpenRouter adds two traps. **Setting `provider.order` disables sticky routing**, which is what keeps repeated prefixes on the provider that holds the cache. Automatic `cache_control` works only when Sonnet is routed to Anthropic directly, not Bedrock or Vertex, so explicit breakpoints are safer ([OpenRouter prompt caching docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/best-practices/prompt-caching.md)). SPEC 6.1's instruction to "pin the provider in OpenRouter routing so cost is stable" therefore works against SPEC 1b's caching rule. Narrow providers with `require_parameters`, `zdr` and `data_collection: "deny"` instead, and use `session_id` for stickiness ([OpenRouter structured outputs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/structured-outputs); [OpenRouter routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/llms-full.txt)). Calls fired in parallel before the first write lands all miss and all pay the write, so send one warm-up call per tick and model, wait for it, then fan out.

The current code gets none of this yet. `packages/agent-core/src/traders.ts` still calls the Anthropic and OpenAI SDKs directly, as PROGRESS.md expects for step 5. It serializes `{opponent_model, your_wallet, market}` into one user message, so the per-vault wallet comes _before_ the shared market data and no prefix can be shared. It sets `max_tokens: 2000` on Claude and no output cap on OpenAI. Output is the expensive side: at 2,000 output tokens an uncached decision costs **$0.0336**, and a 30-decision day plus gas costs about **$1.09, more than the $0.99 fee**. Any reasoning or "thinking" tokens billed as output add $0.001 per 100 tokens. The worker rewrite must order the prompt as static system prompt → tick snapshot → breakpoint → vault state, cap output near 600 tokens, and turn reasoning off or limit it.

## The 30-decision cap will be the normal day, and the $0.022 alarm sits past the rule it guards

SPEC 1b models a typical day at 20 decisions, but its trigger design makes the cap the default. Each new launch triggers a decision in every vault, so daily decisions equal launches × vaults. The per-vault cap binds whenever Blast sees more than ~30 launches a day. No public Blast v2 launch count exists yet, but peer launchpads routinely exceed that, and Pump.fun peaked at **70,576 tokens in one day** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/331108/business-memes-inside-solana-meme-coin-pump-fun)). The structural fix is to filter and score each launch once, globally, in deterministic code, then fan out to vaults. LLM spend then grows with launches, not launches × vaults.

Gas is not the problem. Blast's own October 2026 mainnet transactions show simple shared-object calls netting **~105k–130k MIST (about $0.0001)**. Swap-like PTBs touching a pool and configs net between a rebate and **~8M MIST** (Cetus buy 2.5M, Aftermath sell 7.95M) ([Blast mainnet e2e deployment log](https://github.com/interest-protocol/blast-v2-contracts/blob/main/deployments/sui/mainnet/2026-10-20-blast-presale-versioned-e2e.json)). The non-refundable storage fee fell from 1% to 0.01% at protocol v138 ([Sui protocol config](https://github.com/MystenLabs/sui/blob/main/crates/sui-protocol-config/src/lib.rs)). The SPEC's $0.03–$0.08/day fits 10–30 trades. The ceiling, where every one of 30 decisions trades, is about $0.10–$0.21/day. The reference gas price of ~100 MIST is inferred from these transactions, not read from RPC. A return to 2023–24 levels would multiply the computation part of gas, which makes this the one gas assumption worth an alarm.

Put these together and the worst-case rule ("≥30% margin on a capped day") is fragile:

| Scenario (30 decisions, $0.99 fee)            | Day cost | Margin    |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------- | --------- |
| No caching, gas $0.08 (SPEC "busy")           | $0.616   | 37.8%     |
| No caching, every decision trades (gas $0.21) | $0.746   | **24.7%** |
| 80% cache hits, gas $0.21                     | $0.522   | 47.3%     |
| 0% hits with caching on, gas $0.08            | $0.694   | **29.9%** |
| No caching, 2,000-token outputs               | $1.088   | **−10%**  |

The SPEC's cost alarm ("above $0.022 per decision, lower the cap or raise the fee") fires too late. Solving the rule for the per-decision limit at gas $0.08 gives (0.99 × 0.7 − 0.08) / 30 = **$0.0204**. At $0.022 a capped day already keeps only ~25%. A full cache failure ($0.0205) also never trips $0.022, so the plan needs a separate hit-rate alarm. The repo already has the right tool: `costAlarmThresholdUsd` and `maxDecisionsForMargin` in `/home/user/blaster/packages/shared/src/economics.ts` derive the threshold from the rule. The worker should call these functions rather than read a hard-coded $0.022, and feed them _measured_ gas at the cap, not $0.08.

## SUI-denominated collection works, but a 2x cap breaks for one vault in five within six months

Fees collected in SUI create two risks the SPEC only half covers. The first is the cap. At **SUI ≈ $0.87 on 3 November 2026**, down ~33% from a late-September high near $1.30 ([CoinGabbar](https://coingabbar.com/en/sui-price-prediction-privacy-news-spark-sui-bounce)), $0.99 is about 1.14 SUI and a 2x cap is about 2.28 SUI. The cap binds after any **50% fall from the approval price**. SUI did more than that from January 2025's $5.35 high ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/sui)) and rose ~100% between its August 2026 low of $0.639 ([CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/prediction/sui-price-prediction/)) and September. With an assumed ~80% annualized volatility, a reflection-model estimate puts the chance of touching −50% at about **8% within 90 days, 20% within 180 and 35–40% within a year**. A 3x cap cuts those to about 1%, 5% and 15%. These figures are model estimates, not observed data. When the cap binds, `decideDailyFee` in `/home/user/blaster/packages/shared/src/pricing.ts` charges the cap and sets `capped: true`, a quiet undercharge. That is safe for users but invisible to finance unless it is surfaced. Re-approval through `set_fee_terms` should start well before the cap binds, not after.

The second is treasury currency risk, which the SPEC does not mention. Revenue arrives in SUI, but OpenRouter credits are bought in USDC and infrastructure bills are in USD. A treasury that holds a month of fees through a five-week, 33% drawdown has lost a third of its contribution. Converting treasury SUI to USDC regularly is a funds movement. Under CLAUDE.md it needs explicit approval, so the code should _propose_ a conversion and alert, not execute one.

The collection mechanics are sound. The on-chain `charge_fee` asserts the agent is authorized and the vault is unpaused, the amount is at most `daily_fee_cap`, a new UTC day has started, and the vault has enough SUI, and it pays only `vault.treasury`. Batching is cheap: Sui allows **1,024 commands and 2,048 input objects per PTB** ([Sui protocol config](https://github.com/MystenLabs/sui/blob/main/crates/sui-protocol-config/src/lib.rs)), so one transaction can charge roughly 200–400 vaults at about $0.001 of gas each. For the USD price, a Pyth pull update can be read atomically in the same PTB (SUI/USD feed `0x50c67b3f…a266`) ([Sui docs](https://docs.sui.io/onchain-finance/oracles/consuming-price-feeds)). The existing off-chain median check (two or more sources, 5-minute freshness, 2% spread) is adequate as long as it also clamps day-over-day moves, because the on-chain cap bounds the worst operator error at about one extra fee per vault per day.

## Fixed costs run $4,000–$4,500 a month, and referral payouts double the break-even

The SPEC's $3,100/month undercounts by about a third. Infrastructure at ~$1,000/month holds up to about 500 vaults; the line-item sum is roughly **$530 expected at 100 vaults and $1,000 at 500**. But the RPC line scales with vault count, and two platform facts constrain it. Sui's JSON-RPC is decommissioned, and Sui tells production apps to run a full node or buy a dedicated gRPC or GraphQL endpoint ([Sui JSON-RPC migration guide](https://docs.sui.io/develop/accessing-data/json-rpc-migration)). Shinami scheduled its Sui node service to sunset on 1 June 2026 ([Shinami FAQ](https://docs.shinami.com/help-center/billing/sui-faq)). If each of 2,000 vaults is polled every 60 seconds, that is 260–520M requests a month: **$1,130–$3,720** on Chainstack's request-priced Business plan ([Chainstack](https://chainstack.com/best-sui-rpc-providers-2026/)), versus about $1/M on Ankr's gRPC pricing ([Dwellir](https://www.dwellir.com/blog/best-sui-rpc-providers)). Batched multi-gets plus one checkpoint stream cut that one hundredfold. A hidden Redis trap sits alongside: Upstash itself warns that BullMQ polls Redis even when idle and recommends a fixed plan. At **$0.20 per 100K commands** ([Upstash BullMQ](https://upstash.com/docs/redis/integrations/bullmq); [Upstash billing](https://upstash.com/docs/redis/overall/billing)), pay-as-you-go would cost $860–$1,720/month at 2,000 vaults. KMS (about $1 per key plus $0.15 per 10k signs) ([AWS KMS pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/kms/pricing/)) and Enoki (paid tier from $100 for 400k sponsored transactions) ([Enoki pricing](https://enoki.mystenlabs.com/pricing)) are rounding errors.

The $25k audit is a sound midpoint. Tier-1 per-line rates with a Move premium give about $9k–$32k ([Zealynx](https://www.zealynx.io/research/audit-ops/audit-pricing-2026); [Weiblocks](https://weiblocks.io/smart-contract-audit-cost-guide-2026/)), and OtterSec reportedly quotes $20k–$150k with 4–10 week queues. Treat it as one-off capex, though, and reserve $5k–$15k for a re-review after each upgrade that touches `charge_fee` or the caps. The missing line is a legal opinion on MiCA status. Our planning estimate is €5k–€30k one-off; no firm quote was found. That adds ~$1,350/month amortized and brings the expected total to **$4,000–$4,500/month**.

Break-even depends on which day is typical and whether referrers take a cut. Contribution per paid vault-month, and the vaults needed to cover $3,100 or $4,400 of fixed cost:

| Scenario                             | Daily cost | Contribution/month | Break-even ($3.1k / $4.4k) | With 30% referral share |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------- | ------------------ | -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| SPEC typical: 20 decisions, no cache | $0.41      | $17.49             | 177 / 252                  | 361 / 513               |
| At cap: 30 decisions, no cache       | $0.62      | $11.23             | 276 / 392                  | 1,333 / 1,892           |
| 20 decisions, 80% cache hits         | $0.26      | $21.96             | 141 / 200                  | 237 / 337               |
| At cap, 80% cache hits               | $0.39      | $17.94             | 173 / 245                  | 343 / 487               |

The referral column is decisive. Memecoin bots pay **25–35% of fees** to first-tier referrers ([Trojan docs](https://docs.trojan.com/trojan-arena/referral-system); [CoinCodeCap](https://coincodecap.com/all-telegram-trading-bot-referral-programs-listed)), and SUI Sniper Bot offers up to 35% ([listing](https://entryfun.pages.dev/bots/sui-sniper-bot)). Matching that share of _revenue_ on an uncached, capped vault leaves about $2/month. Pay referrals as a share of contribution, or cap them near 15–20%.

The largest swing line sits outside these tables. MiCA lists portfolio management of crypto-assets as a licensed CASP service modelled on MiFID II ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/cy/pdf/2025/markets-in-crypto-assets-regulation-mica.pdf); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/04/how-is-mica-impacting-fund-managers)). A typical EU authorisation costs **€300k–€700k in year one and €150k–€250k a year after** ([Itispay](https://itispay.com/blog/mica-casp-license-cost)), and Portugal's transitional regime ended on 1 July 2026 ([Vantegris](https://vantegris.com/blog/portugal-crypto-licence/)). No guidance addresses an AI agent with discretion over a self-custodied vault. The non-custodial "agent can never withdraw" design is the main argument against CASP status, which is one more reason to keep it inviolable. The flat fee also beats a profit share on conflict optics, but no MiCA rule makes flat fees lighter. If the opinion goes the wrong way, break-even jumps to thousands of vaults, and the EU becomes a geo-block, not a market.

## The $250 vault pays a 144.5% annual fee, and churn decides LTV

The business works on paper only if users stay, and the price makes that hard for small vaults. $0.99 × 365 is **$361/yr: 144.5% of a $250 vault, 36% of $1,000 and 7.2% of $5,000**. The agent must earn about **+12% a month before fees** for a $250 user to break even, and that is before Blast's own trade fees. The documented defaults are **150 bps of quote per trade, half to Blast and half to the creator**, plus a creator-set sell-burn of up to 50% ([Blast launchpad docs](https://github.com/interest-protocol/blast-v2-contracts/blob/main/docs/llms/launchpad.md)). The 28 October publish readback showed all zeros ([canonical deployment](https://github.com/interest-protocol/blast-v2-contracts/blob/main/deployments/sui/mainnet/2026-10-28-canonical-launch-stack.json)), so the live values need an on-chain read. If the defaults are live, a round trip costs the vault ~3% before slippage. Against that, the outcome data is harsh: in March 2026 **49–50.6% of ~1.4M pump.fun wallets lost money** and most of the rest made under $500 ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/pump-fun-data-shows-49-of-march-traders-in-the-red-as-platform-locks-fees/)). Over six months to mid-2025, about **96% made under $500 or lost** ([HODL FM](https://news.hodlfm.io/earn/pump-fun-trader-losses-data-analysis/)). The profitable share swung from 30% to over 70% by month ([CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)), so retention will follow the market regime.

On price per month, Blast Agents is competitive. $29.70 sits beside Cryptohopper at $29 and Coinrule Investor at $29.99, and under Senpi's current $50 ([Senpi](https://senpi.ai/); [Koinly](https://koinly.io/de/blog/best-crypto-trading-bots/)). Its real competitor, though, is the memecoin bot that charges **about 1% per trade and nothing when idle** ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/telegram-trading-bots-2026-guide)). The flat fee only beats 1% per trade once annual notional passes $36k: about six round trips a month on $250, but just 0.3 on $5,000. Survey data says users will delegate only a slice of their funds. **54% would allow AI no more than 20% of their balance**, and willingness rises to 71.8% when loss caps and auto-stops exist ([MEXC](https://blog.mexc.com/press-release/mexc-ai-trading-report-85-use-ai-for-crypto/)). A $250 vault therefore implies a small portfolio, and its owner feels a visible daily debit that per-trade users never see.

No published churn exists for trading bots or AI agents. Consumer subscriptions under $25 ARPA churn about **6–7% a month** ([ChartMogul](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/203-chart-customer-churn-rate)), and finance apps keep roughly 2–12% of users at D30 ([Enable3](https://enable3.io/blog/app-retention-benchmarks-2025)). Our planning range for a product that loses most users money is **15–40% monthly churn**. That range is an assumption, to be replaced by arena cohorts. At $17.49 contribution, LTV is about **$117 at 15%, $70 at 25% and $44 at 40%**, before trial costs for users who never convert. Crypto CAC for a first-deposit user runs **$150–$300** ([HypeLab](https://www.hypelab.com/blog/crypto-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-2026)), and vetted micro-KOLs reached **$42 CPA** in one case study ([ForkOff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/influencer-marketing/how-to-vet-crypto-kol-2026)). Paid acquisition at $150 needs churn below about **11.6%** (17.49 / 150). So growth has to come from referral and revenue-share channels, which pay only out of revenue, plus the leaderboard. The trial is cheap: about $1.20–$1.90 per signup across typical and capped days. It is also Sybil-able, because zkLogin wallets are free and the $250 deposit can be withdrawn on day four. That cost is bounded, but trial-to-paid conversion should be tracked per cohort.

## Conclusion

The cost side of Blast Agents is a solved problem once three code-level guards land: global launch scoring, an output-token cap and enforced caching. With those, a capped vault costs about $0.39 a day against $0.99, and SPEC 1b's 30% worst-case rule holds with room to spare. Its numbers fail mainly because the $0.022 alarm and the 20-decision "typical" day were chosen by hand rather than derived. The repo's `economics.ts` can already derive both, and the worker should take its thresholds from there, not from SPEC prose.

What actually decides profitability is outside the cost model. At realistic churn, LTV sits below paid CAC, so the business grows only through revenue-share channels and visible agent performance, and break-even is a 250–400-vault problem, not 185. The flat fee is safer legally and predictable for users, but on a $250 vault it is a 144% annual drag that guarantees most users see red. The highest-leverage pricing move is to match the model to the vault size, not to tune the fee. The highest-leverage non-engineering spend is a MiCA opinion before user deposits open, since it is the only line item that could change the answer from "thin but positive" to "not viable in the EU".
